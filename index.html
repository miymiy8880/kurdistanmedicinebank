Who we are

A bridge between full shelves and empty ones.

Kurdistan Medicine Bank exists because the region's shortage of medicine has never really been a shortage — it's a distribution problem. Surplus and donated medicine sits in warehouses abroad while clinics in Kurdistan run short of the basics: insulin, antibiotics, blood pressure medication, asthma inhalers.

We built the logistics, customs, and clinic relationships to close that gap — sourcing medicine, verifying it, and getting it into the hands of the pharmacists and doctors who see patients every day.

"We don't run a pharmacy. We keep a hundred and twenty other ones stocked."

What we do

Four ways we get medicine to the people who need it.

Prescription access

We supply chronic-care medication — for diabetes, hypertension, and heart disease — to partner pharmacies at no cost to the patient.

Clinic partnerships

We stock rural and refugee-camp clinics directly, matching inventory to what each clinic actually treats.

Emergency relief

When flooding, displacement, or conflict cuts off supply lines, we run rapid shipments of trauma and first-response medicine.

Chronic disease support

We help patients with long-term conditions stay on treatment, instead of rationing pills between shipments.
